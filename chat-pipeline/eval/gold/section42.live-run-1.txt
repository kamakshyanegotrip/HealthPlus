**Readiness and eating are one question, not two**

Published pre-operative assessment standards for elective total knee arthroplasty set out glycaemic, cardiovascular and weight-related parameters that the assessing surgical and anaesthetic team reviews before a patient is listed for surgery [[claim:a1000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001]]. Those are the same three domains your diet would be aimed at, which is why I am not treating nutrition as a separate topic — what the assessing team says about the glycaemic and weight parameters is what determines whether diet becomes a pre-surgery task for you, and how long that task takes.

On the dietary side: published nutrition therapy guidance for type 2 diabetes describes several eating patterns, including a vegetarian pattern built on pulses, whole grains, nuts and non-starchy vegetables, as options a dietitian may work from [[claim:a1000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000002]]. You have told us you follow a vegetarian diet, so the nutrition guidance here is drawn from published vegetarian patterns rather than general ones — I withheld a general-population nutrition item on that basis. If we have your dietary pattern wrong, tell me and I'll show the other material.

Two questions to put, in one appointment if you can: "Against the pre-operative criteria this hospital uses, where do I currently stand, and what would need to change before you would list me?" and "Which of these published patterns fits my situation, and what should I be aiming for before surgery?" — the first to the surgeon, the second to a dietitian or your treating doctor. The answer to the first sets your surgery date, and every date below is downstream of it.

**Exercise before surgery and rehabilitation after it are the same programme**

You've told us your mobility is limited, so I'm putting the published precautions ahead of the general advice. Published activity precautions for adults with limited mobility awaiting lower-limb arthroplasty caution against high-impact loading and against unsupervised progression of weight-bearing exercise, and note that a supervised assessment should precede any programme; the evidence band on this item is medium [[claim:a1000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000004]]. Separately, general activity recommendations for adults describe accumulating moderate aerobic activity across the week alongside twice-weekly strengthening work [[claim:a1000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000005]]. Which of those precautions apply to you is not something I can determine — ask your treating doctor or a physiotherapist: "Given my mobility, which of these published precautions apply to me, and what may I safely do before and after surgery?"

That same physiotherapist relationship continues on the other side of the operation. The published recovery course after total knee arthroplasty describes an inpatient phase, a supervised rehabilitation phase measured in weeks, and review points at which progress is formally assessed [[claim:a1000000-0000-4000-8000-00000000000c]]. My sources do not give the number of weeks; for that you need the hospital's own published rehabilitation protocol or the full guideline text, and you should get it in writing before you book flights, because the length of that phase sets your visa term, your accommodation and your return date.

**Visa and fitness to fly are two separate gates, in that order**

The destination's published medical visa route requires an invitation or treatment letter from a recognised hospital, is issued for a stated treatment period, and provides for a separate attendant visa for accompanying family members [[claim:a1000000-0000-4000-8000-00000000000a]]. The stated treatment period is the pressure point: if the letter names a period shorter than the published supervised rehabilitation phase [[claim:a1000000-0000-4000-8000-00000000000c]], you will be asked to leave mid-rehabilitation or to extend in-country. Resolve that before booking — ask the hospital to state the full rehabilitation period in the treatment letter. Given your limited mobility, the attendant visa provision is worth using rather than leaving as an afterthought.

The visa lets you enter; it says nothing about whether you may board. Published aviation medicine criteria set out the post-operative intervals and the mobility, oxygenation and thromboembolic-risk factors an assessing clinician weighs when considering fitness
